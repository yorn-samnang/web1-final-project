Khmer Cuisine

Eat Your Way Through Cambodia

From street-side noodle bowls at dawn to fragrant curries served in banana leaves — Cambodian food is honest, aromatic, and quietly unforgettable.

Food to Try

Taste Khmer dishes worth remembering

Food is one of the most memorable parts of visiting Cambodia. These are the dishes that keep travellers talking long after they've left.

Main Dish

Fish Amok

A classic Khmer dish with curry flavour, coconut notes, and a soft, mousse-like texture steamed in banana leaves.

Main Dish

Beef Lok Lak

Tender wok-tossed beef served over rice with fresh salad and the iconic Cambodian pepper-lime dipping sauce.

Soup

Kuy Teav

A comforting pork or beef broth with rice noodles, herbs, and crispy shallots — Phnom Penh's favourite breakfast.

Noodles

Nom Banh Chok

Fresh hand-ground rice noodles topped with green curry, banana flower, and crunchy seasonal herbs.

Street Snack

Banh Chao

A crispy turmeric rice pancake filled with pork, shrimp, and sprouts — Cambodia's answer to a sizzling crêpe.

Main Dish

Samlor Machu

A tangy, tamarind-soured soup with fish or pork, pineapple, and tomatoes — refreshing and deeply Khmer.

Street Snack

Bai Sach Chrouk

Thin slices of grilled pork marinated in coconut milk served over broken rice — the simplest, best street breakfast.

Drink

Sugarcane Juice

Ice-cold, pressed fresh at roadside carts — the most refreshing way to cool down in the Cambodian heat.

Food to Try in Local Provinces

Siem Reap

The food of Siem Reap:

Main Dish

Prahok

Prahok is a fermented fish paste from Cambodia, used mainly as a seasoning to add strong flavour to dishes.

Main Dish

Fish Amok

A classic Khmer dish with curry flavour, coconut notes, and a soft, mousse-like texture steamed in banana leaves.

Main Dish

Lab Khmer

Lap Khmer is a lime-marinated beef salad with herbs and spices, often served with rice and chili for extra flavour.

Main Dish

Prahok Ktiss

A flavourful dish made with fermented fish paste, served over rice with fresh herbs and vegetables.

Street Snack

Kralan (Bamboo Sticky Rice)

Kralan is sticky rice mixed with beans and coconut water, cooked inside bamboo over fire for a rich, smoky flavour.

Street Snack

Num Banh Chok Teuk Prahok

A traditional Cambodian dish of fresh rice noodles served with fermented fish sauce (prahok) and fresh vegetables like cucumber, cabbage, and water lily stems.

Dessert

Num Akor

Nom Akor is a soft, fluffy steamed cake made from rice flour, palm sugar, and coconut milk, often topped with grated coconut and enjoyed as a popular Cambodian street snack.

Drink

Tuk Tnoat Ju

Tuk Tnoat Ju is a traditional Cambodian drink made from sugar palm nectar, enjoyed fresh for a sweet taste or fermented for a slightly sour flavour.

Food to Try in Local Provinces

Battambang

The Food of Battambang:

Main Dish

Grilled Salted Fish

Grilled Salted Fish is a simple dish of whole fish stuffed with herbs, coated in salt, and charcoal-grilled until crispy outside and tender inside.

Street Snack

Num Krok

Fried coconut and rice flour cakes cooked in a cast-iron pan with moulds over a charcoal fire, available in many delicious variations.

Street Snack

Son Vac

Fish paste grilled in banana leaves, eaten with herbs, noodles, and tangy sauce.

Street Snack

Pon Tia Kon

Boiled balut — first drink the warm savory broth from the shell, then season with salt, pepper, and fresh lime juice.

Street Snack

Sach Ko Ang

Grilled beef skewers, juicy from added fat and flavoured with lemongrass kroeung, giving a sweet and aromatic taste loved by locals and tourists.

Street Snack

Lort Cha

Stir-fried rice noodles cooked with soy sauce, fish sauce, vegetables, and pork, often topped with a fried egg and chili.

Dessert

Banh Ja'Neuk

Rice-flour balls stuffed with mung bean paste, drowned in coconut milk and tapioca, doused with ginger syrup, and sprinkled with sesame seeds.

Foods & Drinks to Try at Cambodia's Beach Islands

Koh Rong, Koh Rong Samloem, Kompong Som, Kampot

The Food of Cambodia's Beach Islands:

Main Dish

Grilled Squid (Ang Dtray-meuk)

Fresh squid grilled over charcoal and served with lime and Kampot pepper sauce — smoky, tender, and slightly sweet.

Main Dish

BBQ Whole Fish

Locally caught fish (snapper or barracuda) grilled whole with herbs, garlic, and salt — juicy and full of flavour.

Main Dish

Garlic Butter Prawns

Large prawns sautéed in garlic and butter — simple but incredibly flavourful.

Main Dish

Crab with Kampot Pepper

A signature Cambodian dish using famous pepper from Kampot — spicy, aromatic, and rich.

Main Dish

Seafood Fried Rice

A classic quick meal with egg, fresh vegetables, and a generous mix of local seafood.

Drink

Fresh Coconut Water

Refreshingly sweet and hydrating, perfect for beating the tropical heat.

Drink

Fruit Juice

Freshly blended tropical fruits — mango, watermelon, pineapple — served ice-cold at beachside shacks.

Drink

Beach Cocktails

Mojitos, piña coladas, or local rum mixes — best enjoyed during sunset by the sea.

Street Food Culture

Eat Like a Local

The street is where the best food lives

Cambodia's most authentic flavours aren't in fancy restaurants. They're in the woks of market vendors who've been cooking the same dish for 30 years. Here's what to look for:

  • Early Morning Markets Breakfast crowds gather from 5–8 am. Kuy Teav, Bai Sach Chrouk, and fresh fruit are best eaten here.
  • Night Market Grills As the sun sets, BBQ skewers, grilled corn, and sticky rice fill the air around Phnom Penh's riverside.
